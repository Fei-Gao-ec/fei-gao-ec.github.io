Lag-1 V may be mechanical. This is a proposition in reference_case.tex, restated in line 21. If the return is independent of the stake and of everything known when that stake was placed, then

\[\mathbb{E}[b_t-b_{t-k}\mid r_t]=\mathbb{E}[b_t\mid r_t]-\mathbb{E}[b_{t-k}]\]

for every lag \(k\ge 1\). A reference update changes \(\mathbb{E}[b_t\mid r_t]\), and every lag inherits that change. A twist that exists only at lag 1 is then \(\mathbb{E}[b_{t-1}\mid RR_t]\), the stake that produced the observed return. The same reply said the lag-1 versus lag-2 comparison cannot support “the reference updates slowly,” because lag 2 is smoother for mechanical reasons. The user did not retract that. The lag-1 model they set aside makes the same point in another way: the V is selection of who placed \(b_{t-1}\), not a cut in the new stake \(b_t\).
